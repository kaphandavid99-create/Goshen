1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Goshen Provision Project represents a comprehensive digital transformation solution for a traditional provision shop operating in New Bell, Bamenda, Cameroon. This document serves as the complete system-design and implementation record submitted in support of the project defense.

The solution consists of a responsive Progressive Web Application (PWA) that functions seamlessly across desktop and mobile devices, providing customers with the ability to browse products online, place orders remotely, track shipments, and enjoy loyalty rewards. The platform serves multiple customer segments: retail customers, wholesale buyers, and internal staff management.

Key Achievements

  • Developed a fully functional e-commerce platform with 12 core features
  • Designed and implemented a robust relational database with 15 interconnected tables
  • Created an intuitive user interface for both customers and administrators
  • Integrated multiple payment methods including Mobile Money and Cash-on-Delivery
  • Implemented AI-powered shopping assistant for 24/7 customer support
  • Built loyalty and referral programs to encourage repeat business
  • Deployed push notification system for customer engagement

Business Impact

By transforming from a location-dependent business model to a digital-first approach, Goshen Provision is now accessible to customers throughout Bamenda, regardless of physical location or foot traffic. The platform enables the business to reach customers beyond those who can physically visit the shop, directly addressing the primary business challenge of increased local competition from business relocations.

2. PROBLEM GIVEN BY THE PROJECT OWNER

2.1 Background of the Problem

The project was initiated based on a business challenge experienced by the owner of Goshen Provision, located in New Bell, Bamenda. Bamenda has been experiencing significant road construction and related changes in the movement of people and businesses. As a result, some businesses operating in affected areas have had to relocate to other locations. Some of these businesses have moved to areas around the location of Goshen Provision.

The movement of several businesses into the same area has increased the level of competition and congestion within the business environment. As more businesses offer similar products and services in the same area, Goshen Provision has experienced a marked reduction in customer traffic and overall business activity.

2.2 Main Problem Identified

"The relocation of businesses caused by road construction has increased the concentration of businesses around Goshen Provision, resulting in increased competition and a reduction in the number of customers visiting the shop."

The business therefore needs a way to reach customers beyond relying only on people who physically pass by or visit the shop. Traditional marketing approaches and physical location advantages are no longer sufficient in this increasingly competitive environment.

2.3 Effects of the Problem

The identified problem has manifested in several significant effects on the business:

Effect Description Business Impact
Reduced Customer Traffic Fewer customers physically visit the shop compared to pre-construction periods Lower daily sales volume and reduced impulse purchases
Increased Competition More businesses operating in the same area selling similar products Price pressure and customer fragmentation
Reduced Visibility With many businesses competing, it becomes harder to differentiate Declining market share in local area
Physical Customer Dependency Business model relies entirely on walk-in foot traffic Vulnerability to external factors affecting foot traffic
Limited Geographic Reach Customers outside immediate area unaware of offerings Lost opportunities to serve broader market

2.4 Problem to be Solved

The project focuses on solving the problem of limited customer reach caused by increased business congestion and competition resulting from the relocation of businesses due to road construction. The proposed system provides Goshen Provision with a digital platform through which customers can discover available products, place orders remotely, and choose between delivery and collection options. This enables the business to reach customers without depending entirely on physical visits to the shop.

3. SOLUTIONS IMPLEMENTED

3.1 Solution Overview

Rather than attempting to compete on location or price alone in an increasingly crowded retail environment, the solution implements a digital-first, omnichannel strategy. This approach creates a permanent digital storefront that exists independently of physical foot traffic, reaches customers wherever they are, provides multiple fulfillment options, builds customer loyalty through rewards programs, and enables expansion into new customer segments and geographic areas.

The system was built as a responsive Progressive Web App (PWA) that:

  • Runs in the browser like a website but installs on phones like a native app
  • Works online and offline with intelligent synchronization
  • Provides fast, responsive performance across all devices
  • Supports push notifications for customer engagement
  • Integrates with local payment methods (Cash-on-Delivery, Mobile Money)
  • Serves both English and French-speaking customers

3.2 How the Solution Addresses Each Effect

Each effect of the problem identified in Section 2.3 is addressed directly by a specific part of the implemented system:

4. CORE FEATURES IMPLEMENTATION

4.1 The Twelve Core Features

Feature 1: Online Product Catalog

Every item Goshen sells is listed with its price, unit, category, and photo. Products are searchable and filterable by category or current deals. This digital equivalent of browsing shelves is available to customers regardless of foot traffic congestion around the physical shop. Live stock status is displayed, allowing customers to know availability before placing orders.

Feature 2: Cart, Checkout & Fulfillment Choice

Customers build a cart and check out with the option to choose either home delivery or in-store pickup. The checkout process displays the order total and delivery fee upfront, with real-time recalculation based on fulfillment choice. This transparency builds customer trust and reduces cart abandonment.

Feature 3: Cash & Mobile Money Payment

Orders can be paid in two ways: Cash on delivery/pickup (familiar to Bamenda customers) or immediately by Mobile Money (MTN). This dual-payment approach accommodates existing payment preferences while encouraging immediate payment for those who prefer it.

Feature 4: Loyalty Points Program

Customers earn points for signing up (100 pts), every purchase (1.5 pts per 100 FCFA), and product reviews (10 pts). Points are redeemable as discounts at checkout (1 pt = 1 FCFA off, minimum 500 pts). This creates financial incentives for repeat business and engagement.

Feature 5: Referral Program

Existing customers earn 100 points when a friend signs up via their referral link, and another 100 points when that friend places their first order. This turns the customer base into an organic marketing channel while rewarding word-of-mouth growth.

Feature 6: Push & In-App Notifications

Order updates, abandoned-cart reminders, loyalty nudges, and staff-issued promotions are delivered directly to customer devices. This keeps Goshen visible even when customers are not actively shopping, addressing the reduced visibility problem directly.

Feature 7: AI Shopping Assistant

A 24/7 chat assistant answers questions about stock, order status, delivery times, and loyalty points instantly, without staff involvement. This replaces the shop attendant for remote customers who would otherwise need to call or visit in person.

Feature 8: Installable Web & Mobile App

The platform installs to phone or desktop home screens like a native app, keeping Goshen visible long after customers leave the page. This persistent presence directly counters the reduced visibility caused by local competition.

Feature 9: Wholesale Ordering Portal

Approved business buyers can apply for wholesale pricing and place bulk orders. This opens a second customer segment unaffected by retail foot traffic competition, directly extending reach beyond individual walk-in customers.

Feature 10: Bilingual Interface

The entire platform is available in both English and French, serving the mixed-language environment of Bamenda. This doubles the addressable customer base compared to a single-language platform.

Feature 11: Admin Management Dashboard

The shop owner manages products, orders, wholesale applications, and promotions from a single password-protected dashboard. Live KPIs display revenue, order counts, inventory status, and customer analytics. This replaces multiple manual processes with one centralized system.

Feature 12: Customer Reviews & Wishlist

Verified buyers can rate and review products (earning 10 loyalty points), building trust for remote customers. Wishlists keep customer interest alive between visits, reducing the churn that comes from one-time shopping apps.

5. DATABASE DESIGN & MYSQL DDL SCRIPTS

5.1 Database Technology Selection

The system uses PostgreSQL, an open-source relational database hosted on Supabase. PostgreSQL was chosen for four critical reasons:

  • Data Integrity for Money and Stock: Orders, payments, and loyalty points must never be lost, duplicated, or inconsistent. PostgreSQL enforces this through ACID transactions and foreign-key constraints.
  • Inherently Relational Data Model: A customer has many orders, an order has many items, items refer to products, products belong to categories. This natural relationship structure is exactly what relational databases optimize for.
  • Mature Hosting at Low Cost: PostgreSQL is widely supported by affordable managed hosts like Supabase, appropriate for small-business projects with limited operating budgets.
  • Flexibility Where Needed: JSON column types store variable-structure fields (like drink flavour options) without sacrificing relational integrity elsewhere in the schema.

5.2 Object-Relational Mapper (Prisma)

The application talks to the database through Prisma, an ORM tool, rather than raw SQL. Prisma was chosen because it generates fully type-checked database clients from a single schema file, tracks every schema change as a versioned migration file, automatically parameterizes queries to eliminate SQL injection, and reads like the relationships it represents (e.g., "user.orders.find()" is more readable than a hand-written join).

5.4 Database Tables Overview

The schema consists of 15 tables organized by feature area:

  • User Management (1 table): User - Core identity table for all accounts
  • Product Catalog (2 tables): Category, Product - Browse and discovery
  • Orders (4 tables): Order, OrderItem, Payment, Address - Complete order lifecycle
  • Cart (1 table): CartSnapshot - Server-side cart mirroring
  • Notifications (2 tables): Notification, PushSubscription - Messaging and delivery
  • AI Assistant (2 tables): ChatThread, ChatMessage - Conversation history
  • Engagement (2 tables): Review, WishlistItem - Trust and retention
  • Wholesale (1 table): WholesaleApplication - Business buyer management

5.5 Database Relationships

User sits at the center of the schema because almost every feature ultimately belongs to one customer account. This design ensures that a person's orders, loyalty balance, referrals, notifications, and reviews all key off the same id, making it possible to recognize a returning customer across every feature of the system.

Core Relationships:

  • User (1) → Order (many): every order a customer has placed
  • User (1) → Review (many): every product rating a customer has submitted
  • User (1) → WishlistItem (many): products saved for later
  • User (1) → Notification (many): messages delivered to that customer
  • User (1) → ChatThread (many): conversation history with the AI assistant
  • Order (1) → OrderItem (many): line items in the order
  • Order (1) → Payment (1): exactly one payment per order
  • Product (1) → OrderItem (many): every time a product was ordered
  • Product (1) → Review (many): ratings from customers
  • Category (1) → Product (many): products grouped by type

6. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & TECHNOLOGY STACK

6.1 Frontend Architecture

Next.js Framework

React-based meta-framework for production web applications. Provides server-side rendering and static generation for performance, file-based routing system, and built-in API routes for backend functionality.

TypeScript

Brings static typing to JavaScript, catching errors at development time rather than in production. Enables improved IDE support and autocomplete, with type signatures serving as inline documentation.

Tailwind CSS

Utility-first CSS framework providing pre-built design tokens for colors, spacing and sizing. Includes responsive design utilities and compiles to a minimal CSS bundle.

React Query

Efficient data fetching and caching library. Provides automatic refetching and revalidation, with perfect fit for e-commerce apps where product data and inventory change frequently.

6.2 Backend Technologies

Next.js API Routes

Serverless functions in the /api directory with automatic scaling and no server management required. Application business logic runs in these routes without needing a separate backend framework.

Prisma ORM

Type-safe database access with automatic migration management. Query builder includes TypeScript support, and schema visualization tools aid in understanding data relationships.

PostgreSQL Database

Open-source relational database with ACID compliance ensuring data integrity. Foreign key constraints maintain referential integrity, JSON support handles flexible fields, and excellent performance for relational queries.

Supabase Hosting

Managed PostgreSQL in the cloud with built-in authentication support, real-time subscriptions capability, and automatic backups with scalable hosting.

6.3 External Integrations

Cloudinary

Manages product photos and customer avatars with CDN delivery for fast image loading. Automatic image optimization and resizing via URL-based transformations.

MTN Mobile Money API

Integration for Cameroon's dominant mobile payment method. API calls initiate and confirm payments with webhook callbacks when transactions complete, using reference IDs for reconciliation.

Google Maps

Embedded maps on the Contact page pinpoint store location. Integration with the customer's own maps app for turn-by-turn navigation directions.

Web Push API

Browser push notifications to customer devices. Service Workers handle background notifications, with user subscription management for permission and opt-in.

6.4 Development Tools & Practices

npm Package Manager

Dependency management and script running. Critical: always use `npm install --include=dev` in this project to preserve development dependencies.

Git Version Control

Code versioning with pull request review workflow. Issue tracking and project management integrated.

Type Safety with TypeScript

Prevents entire classes of runtime errors. ESLint catches code quality issues, Prettier auto-formats for consistency.

Testing Framework

Unit tests with Jest or Vitest. Integration tests for API routes. End-to-end tests for critical user flows.
